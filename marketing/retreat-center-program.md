# Anjoy Retreat Center — Facility & Service Program (Lori / Gavar)

> What the center should physically contain *beyond* practice spaces: heat, water, bodywork.
> Derived from Respace Prime (Moscow) read as a **build spec**, then re-grounded in Armenia.
> Living doc. Last updated: 2026-09-10.

## Why this belongs in the plan (the financial argument)

`/retreat-center` currently promises six facilities: practice hall, nidra room, circle room,
12–16 guest rooms, kitchen, outdoor deck. All six earn money **only while a retreat is running.**
A 12–16 room center realistically runs 20–25 retreat weeks a year; the rest is fixed cost.

A thermal + bodywork layer is one of very few lines that earns in three different modes:

- **inside** the retreat package — raises the price at near-zero marginal cost once built;
- as an **upsell during** a retreat — revenue-strategy 🟢 #3, to a guest already on site;
- with **no retreat and no teacher at all** — day guests, Yerevan weekenders, Sevan summer traffic.

The third mode is the point. Empty weeks are the #1 financial risk of owning a building. The
retreat-hosting model (competitor-analysis › 🟡 #5) fills them with *other teachers*; a thermal
complex fills them with nobody's labour but a stoker's and a therapist's. They are complementary
hedges — plan both.

Scale anchor: Respace charges **14,000 ₽ for three hours of thermal access with no teacher
involved**, and 19,500–60,000 ₽ for a private banya ritual. Armenian prices won't be Moscow
prices, but the ratio holds — heat, water and a skilled attendant are premium-priced everywhere
in this segment, and the guest is already standing in the building.

> **Correction to the earlier read.** competitor-analysis.md first filed the facility stack as
> "not for Anjoy — capex Respace has and Anjoy doesn't." For a business with no building, right.
> For **a center being designed from scratch, backwards**: capex decided at design time is cheap,

⛔ **The pool is the trap.** Fifteen metres of heated water is the largest capex *and* operating
line on the list — pumps, treatment, heating, cover, cleaning staff, statutory water testing — and
it is an *amenity*, not a ritual: no performer, no booking slot, no per-session price. At **Gavar
there is Lake Sevan**; at **Lori there is canyon water**. Build a small hot soaking pool and a cold
plunge, and let the geography do the swimming.

## The product is a sequence, not a room

Respace's whole banya menu — a 20-minute single parenie at 9,000 ₽ through 4-hour mineral rituals
at 60,000 ₽ — is one loop, repeated and dressed:

**heat → cold → rest → tea**, with a master directing it and an aroma / herb / scrub theme on top.

That matters twice.

1. **Cheap to vary, expensive to buy.** A small primitive set — steam, dry heat, cold water, scrub,
   wrap, whisking, oil, herbs, tea, a rest room — composes into a dozen named rituals. Respace's
   five SPA programs (24,000–49,000 ₽) are exactly that: the same treatments resequenced and
   renamed. With one banya master and two therapists, Anjoy can publish a menu that reads far
   bigger than the payroll behind it.
2. **It is already Anjoy's subject.** Hot → cold → rest is nervous-system work: sympathetic load,
   then a parasympathetic drop. That is what nidra, pranayama and the planned Stillness Room are
   *for*. At a city spa the contrast loop is a luxury bought by the hour; on a retreat it is
   **curriculum** — placed in the day's arc (after asana, before nidra), taught rather than sold.

That second point is the real edge over any wellness club. Respace can sell the loop. It cannot
explain it, sequence it across a week, or stand a teacher next to it. Anjoy can.

## The Armenian version — do not transplant the Moscow menu

Copying "Императорская баня" and "Ля Султан де Саба" onto a Lori hillside produces a mediocre
Moscow spa at altitude, beatable by any Moscow club on its own terms. The defensible version is
the one only that land can offer.

Regional assets to design against — **the site-specific ones need Ani; she knows the ground and
has a house near Sevan**:

- **Wood-fired heat.** Lori has forest. A wood-fired stove *is* the authenticity Moscow's electric
  rooms have to buy with marketing — and it is the cheapest heat available there.
- **Cold, at altitude.** Sevan sits ≈1,900 m and is cold most of the year; Lori's canyon streams
  run cold year-round. Free cold plunge, and the altitude makes the contrast loop land differently
  — an honest program claim, not a marketing one.
- **Mountain herbs.** Thyme (ուրց), oregano, mint, chamomile, rosehip, sea buckthorn — for the
  steam infusion, the whisking bundle, the wrap, and the tea room. Local, seasonal, cheap, and a
  story per herb.
- **Volcanic stone.** Tuff and basalt are the region's own building material *and* an obvious
  hot-stone line — Armenian basalt rather than imported Hawaiian.
- **Mineral-water tradition.** Armenia's thermal/mineral spring culture — Jermuk (literally
  "warm spring"), Arzni, Dilijan, Bjni, Hankavan — is a genuine cultural anchor for a mineral
  bathing line. ❓**Ask Ani:** is there a spring on or near either parcel? If yes it reshapes the
  whole build; if no, mineral bathing becomes a sourced-water product — still viable.
- **Bathhouse culture.** The Caucasus bath tradition (and its Tbilisi sulphur-bath cousin, a few
  hours from Lori) is a stronger regional root for the warm room than "Turkish bath" framing.
- **The table.** Lavash, honey, apricot, herbal tea, Areni wine — the rest room and tea ritual
  write themselves, and they connect straight to the farm-to-table kitchen already promised.

Naming follows the rule the rest of the brand follows: Armenian-rooted and true, never orientalist
pastiche.

## Proposed facility program

**Wet / thermal block** — design as one zone sharing plant, drainage and ventilation
1. Wood-fired steam room (Russian-style), 60–90 °C — the anchor
2. Dry sauna, 70–90 °C — always-on, cheapest heat per guest
3. Cold plunge — ideally fed by, or opening onto, the natural cold (stream / lake)
4. Warm outdoor soaking pool with the view — this is the photograph that sells the center
5. Rest & tea room — generously sized; where the effect lands and where guests buy
6. Outdoor showers, changing and dry zone
7. *(Phase 2)* Hammam-style warm room, 40–50 °C, tuff / basalt
8. *(Phase 2)* Vichy / rain treatment room

**Bodywork block**
9. Two treatment rooms to start — plumbed, sound-isolated, one convertible for couples
10. Practitioner-flexible: massage, stone therapy (local basalt), scrubs, wraps
11. *(Later)* third room or flotation, only if occupancy justifies it

**Support — invisible and decisive**
12. Wet-side plant: heat source, water treatment, drainage, ventilation, laundry
13. Linen and product store — a spa runs on towels
14. Staff room for therapists and the banya master

**Movement**
15. Small strength & mobility room (mats, suspension, free weights) — not a gym

## Phasing — what money buys the most, when

**Phase 0 — before or with the first building.** Get the *plumbing decisions* right even if
nothing is fitted out: site the wet block, oversize drainage and heat source, run the services.
Nearly free now, punishing later. **This is the only irreversible item on the page.**

**Phase 1 — the minimum that earns.** Wood-fired steam room + dry sauna + cold plunge + rest/tea
room + **two massage rooms**. A small fraction of Respace's stack that delivers the complete
contrast ritual and the highest-margin service in the building. Two treatment rooms are the
cheapest revenue line here by a wide margin — a room, a table, linens and a trained pair of hands,
against 9,000–46,000 ₽ per session at Respace prices.

**Phase 2 — depth and photographs.** Outdoor soaking pool with the view, hammam warm room,
Vichy/rain room, third treatment room.

**Phase 3 — only on proven demand.** Flotation, mineral bathing suite, anything needing a
technician on call.

## Operating questions to settle at design time, not after

1. **Retreat guests and day guests share one building.** A silent retreat and a Saturday crowd
   from Yerevan cannot occupy the rest room in the same hour. Choose the separation now — split
   hours, split entrances, or public access only between retreats. It constrains the *floor plan*,
   which is exactly why it is a design-time decision.
2. **Mixed-gender thermal space on a retreat.** Scheduled sessions, swimwear norms, or bookable
   private slots — pick a policy and publish it. Guests ask before they book.
3. **The master is a hire, not a fixture.** Respace names and photographs its parmeisters because
   they are a draw. A wood-fired ritual without a skilled person is just a hot room. Finding and
   keeping that person in rural Lori is harder than building the room — start early, and consider
   training someone local.
4. **Staffing floor.** Roughly two therapists + one banya master + a stoker/attendant is the
   smallest team that runs Phase 1 six days a week. Respace carries 12+ specialists for a city
   club; a 12–16 room center needs a fraction of that — but not zero, and not summer-only if the
   between-retreats revenue is meant to be real.
5. **Water and wood.** Volume, rights, winter supply, and a sustainable wood source. Rural, at
   altitude, in winter.
6. **Regulation.** ❓Unverified: licensing for massage/spa services, water-quality rules for shared
   pools, construction permitting in Armenia. Needs local counsel *before* the drawings.
7. **Winter is the argument, not the obstacle.** Lori and Sevan winters are the *best* season for
   a contrast complex — which makes the thermal layer an **off-season anchor** precisely when
   retreat demand is thinnest. Design the outdoor plunge and soaking pool for snow, not just for
   summer photographs.
